# Search and evidence design

Tactical Atlas should help a player reach an evidence-backed shortlist from an ordinary description of how they want to play. A lesser-known game should be discoverable through the same mechanics and evidence as a familiar one. The preview covers turn-based tactical combat, including party RPGs, and gives players an explicit answer when the catalogue cannot establish what they asked for.

The [information requirements brief](INFORMATION_REQUIREMENTS.md) maps the current gaps to player, developer and curator needs and defines what to validate before expanding the system.

## What the November 2 release can do

The release separates language interpretation from ranking. DeepSeek maps the request to editable requirements, preferences, and title exclusions. The matcher compares those criteria against eight structured facets, each with a support status and evidence references. Preferred criteria affect order. A supported match contributes +2, a supported mismatch −2, and an unknown 0, with a player-editable weight of 1 or 3. Provisional judgments receive the same preference contribution as supported facets today. The matcher uses relevant criterion coverage to break score ties before title order. Review counts and sales play no role. [Release checkpoint](RELEASE_2026-11-02.md) · [Matcher](../lib/match.ts) · [Vocabulary](../lib/taxonomy.ts)

The 24-profile release has 61 evidence records, of which 51 are source reports and 10 are provisional assessments. Fifty-three records link to Steam and zero are classified as recorded observations. Narrative emphasis is recorded for 14 titles and unknown for 10. Offline support is unknown for all 24. Ability interactions are source-supported for six, provisional for four, and unknown for 14. The release contains no comparable campaign-hour estimates. These are coverage counts, so a larger catalogue can still leave the same player questions unanswered. [Release catalogue](https://github.com/arcueidbrunnstadt/tactical-atlas/blob/c52d5754972ad4ed4b4c98acbb71c796196a983c/public/catalogue.json)

The release keeps unsupported wording visible but represents it as plain text outside the criteria. An explicit unsupported must can therefore coexist with a match count based on the supported subset. Flat criteria also cannot distinguish “fantasy or science fiction” from a conjunction of separate conditions. The interpretation contract provides a source phrase and clarification choices, while the interface drops the source phrase and shows only the question. The provider request includes all catalogue identities, which limits growth under the 16 KiB request cap. ## Represent the questions and their evidence

Keep a canonical catalogue with stable title identities, edition and platform scope, and shared values across languages. Separate the information that reviewers collect from the search signals derived from it.

1. **Evidence record.** Store an attributable claim or bounded observation with a source link and locator, checked date, basis, edition or build when known, platform where relevant, and limits. A developer statement, a gameplay observation, a published aggregate, and a reviewer inference carry different implications. Store disagreements and stale claims with their scope so a reviewer can resolve them.
2. **Search signal.** Derive a typed property from one or more evidence records. Use categories for properties such as setting, explicit presence or absence for bounded factual claims, scoped estimates or ranges for quantities, and defined observations for qualities with meaningful subdimensions. Record uncertainty and retain evidence IDs. A partial feature inventory is insufficient to establish absence.
3. **Interpreted request.** Preserve the original phrase, required or preferred importance, and how the phrase was represented. Typed criteria can hold a categorical selection, a numeric comparison or a preference target. The local pilot can group simple alternatives under one requirement or preference. Leave material ambiguity open for clarification or manual editing. Keep an unresolved mandatory phrase attached to the query so the shortlist reflects it.

For “long narrative,” separate authored story emphasis from estimated campaign duration. Specify whether the estimate concerns the main story, a named campaign, or completion with optional content. Keep source ranges, sample context when available, and play-style caveats. Duration can help find a substantial campaign. Story structure, pacing and narrative density require their own evidence. Apply the same approach to “deep character building” by showing distinct observations such as branching choices, interactions across systems, and what can be changed later. Overall quality judgments stay unresolved unless the project defines and reviews evidence for a narrower claim.

## Two layers for game information

| Layer | What it keeps | What it is used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source records** | The original material we can retain or a link and precise location, with source identity, retrieval or observation date, and any limits on scope or use. Record successive versions where available. | Preserve context and make it possible to revisit or reuse information as the project changes. |
| **Structured catalogue** | Reviewed properties, estimates and observations, linked to their source records. Each entry has a defined meaning, the applicable game or edition, and platform, version, or other conditions when relevant. It can record uncertainty and conflicting evidence. | Filter and rank games, explain results, and make comparisons using the same underlying claims. |

The structured layer is built from evidence and review decisions. Preserve the relationship from a search result to the claim, the specific source record and the location that supports it. Keep corrections and review decisions with the evidence used to build the structured entries. Recheck or withdraw affected entries and update the search view when a source is superseded or a claim is corrected. A rebuild should preserve those review decisions and handle older records with incomplete fields. Record the date a source was retrieved separately from the date or version it describes. Historical observations can remain useful within their original scope. [W3C provenance overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)

Start with properties that affect recurring decisions and that we can support for multiple games. Give each property a meaning, scope, accepted values or units, and an evidence rule. For example, a duration estimate needs the kind of play it measures, such as reaching a main story ending. The catalogue can retain useful context in text and add more structured properties when the examples justify them.

Start by testing the structured catalogue as the ordinary search path. Source material can support editorial work before a search, such as adding a searchable property, reprocessing an earlier collection, or investigating a gap across titles. It can also support a search by providing context for an explanation or helping find candidates the structured catalogue misses. These are examples of workflows to test. If searching source text during a request looks promising, compare it with the structured path on the same cases. Measure the relevant titles it adds, the errors it introduces, and the cost and response time. A text match may surface a useful lead. A hard match requires evidence for the exact condition.

For the beta, keep the two layers logically separate and start with the existing versioned catalogue and linked evidence records. The data can live in separate collections or tables. Consider separate database services if measured volume, search workload or access needs justify the additional work of synchronizing and operating them. A text index can also be built over stored content without requiring another database service. [SQLite full-text search documentation](https://www.sqlite.org/fts5.html#overview_of_fts5)

Keep the contracts between evidence collection, request interpretation, matching and presentation small and explicit. An added property should define its evidence and matching behavior in one place, with the interface able to show what it means. Keep changes to data formats versioned and check that existing records still work.

Evaluate search methods on the quality of the results they produce. Estimate testing costs and ongoing costs at expected usage, including model calls, infrastructure and review effort. A more expensive method may be worth using if it resolves important requests that cheaper methods miss or saves enough work elsewhere. Use reusable claims and local reranking to avoid repeated work. The current trial cap governs paid testing and operation. Set the future operating budget based on expected usage and demonstrated gains.

## Collect data around player decisions

Start each batch with a small set of player questions. Select contrasting games for which different answers would change a decision. Reuse the same collection checklist across popular and lesser-known games. Seek developer manuals, official descriptions, versioned documentation and scoped gameplay observations. Accept developer contributions as attributed evidence leads. Apply the same review criteria regardless of who supplied a link. Publish original summaries and direct links, using media copies only when their display and redistribution rights are documented. [Contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) · [Artwork sources and licences](ARTWORK.md)

For each proposed field, record what counts as a positive, negative, estimate, or unknown and identify which sources can establish each state. Validate IDs, references, units, ranges, scope and bilingual alignment before publishing. Require review of a claim before it affects a factual hard filter. A scoped source excerpt or timestamp helps players check what actually happens and gives future reviewers a place to start. Time-box the first collection batch and report reviewer time per usable claim and coverage by intent and title cohort. Prioritize questions where another reviewed claim would change a shortlist. Use volunteered or consented research prompts to select those questions and keep raw public requests out of routine analytics.

## Rank by fit with explicit uncertainty

Evaluate hard conditions first. A supported conflict can exclude a game. A condition with missing, provisional, conflicting, or out-of-scope evidence belongs in Needs checking. The confirmed group requires support for each mandatory condition.

For a categorical exclusion, a documented presence is a conflict. Record evidence of absence for the specific category in `absentValues` with a source or observation covering that scope. Existing records that mention other features leave the exclusion unresolved, whether they record one feature or several. If the records support both presence and absence, show the conflict as unresolved. Explicit Boolean values continue to answer their yes-or-no questions.

Progression evidence can cover just part of a game's development system. For this category, both a single recorded value and a list establish only the values they cover. An unlisted requested feature stays open unless scoped evidence establishes its absence. A required feature with incomplete evidence keeps the game in Needs checking. For a preference, the same evidence gap contributes zero to the score.

Within each group, combine per-preference fit with visible weights. The local pilot separates three comparisons: a minimum favors values at or above the target; a maximum favors values at or below it; and an approximate target favors closeness on either side. For a reported range, the scorer uses the endpoint that gives the weaker fit. Both ends of a 30–40-hour estimate are five hours from a 35-hour target, so it earns more proximity credit than a 14-hour estimate. An open-ended lower estimate can inform a minimum, but leaves the maximum and proximity scores unknown. Missing estimates stay unknown too. These rules are reusable for other positive numeric properties once their units, scope and evidence requirements are defined.

The manual duration editor starts with an editable 50-hour minimum. For interpreted duration, validate the comparison against the wording tied to the quoted whole-hour quantity. Leave comparisons or scopes that cannot be represented accurately unresolved. Completionist, DLC and session lengths are separate from the time to reach one story ending. The four developer reports inform preference ranking and leave hard duration conditions open. They do not establish how long a particular player will take.

Track evidence coverage separately from fit. More sources about the same mechanic can increase confidence without counting as additional matches. Treat provisional signals conservatively and preserve near ties where the evidence is weak. For niche visibility, measure the outcomes for equally relevant titles and check whether source gaps keep one cohort out of the confirmed group. A separate, clearly marked view of possible fits gives sparse profiles a path to discovery while keeping the evidence standard consistent.

## Architecture and cost choices

| Approach | Role and tradeoff |
| --- | --- |
| Structured catalogue and deterministic matching | Keep the current browser-side path for the preview. Typed values support comparisons and reproducible explanations. Review and upkeep take work, so add fields only when they answer recurring player questions. |
| Bounded language interpretation | Let the model translate a request to editable intent, with schema and cross-field validation. Keep selection, facts, and reasoning in the application. Resolve exact titles and aliases locally before supplying relevant identities to the provider. Ambiguous title names can be clarified locally. |
| Text or semantic retrieval | Consider this later for finding candidate passages and relevant profiles when full-catalogue matching or the vocabulary becomes a bottleneck. Retrieval similarity can find leads in prose. Claims and hard outcomes still require scoped evidence. An index brings data, evaluation and maintenance costs. |
| Versioned JSON and existing SQLite | Keep reviewed catalogue data and bilingual copy in versioned source for this scale. Retain SQLite for operational data and the single shared ledger. Consider a separate ingestion or retrieval store when update volume, concurrency, or catalogue size justifies it. |

Routine development uses manual entry, local fixtures and fake-provider integration checks with zero paid requests. Provider pricing and currency references require verified review within the existing freshness window. Account readiness and available app allowance are separate checks. Any hosted change must preserve the production volume, prior reservations and cumulative 1,000-cent ceiling. Under the existing 3-cent reservation, the November 2 checkpoint's 961 unreserved cents represented up to 320 whole reservations at that moment. Current usage must come from the authoritative ledger. | 3. Compare local search behavior | Run both versions on fixed catalogue data and fresh bilingual test cases. Use existing project examples and clearly marked synthetic cases. | Show that the targeted failure improves while meeting the hard-condition gates. Freeze queries, acceptable interpretations and judgments about fit before tuning. Use 8–12 initial cases in English and Simplified Chinese, including paraphrases, negation, alternatives, scope differences and cases with insufficient evidence. Prepare a separate set before tuning to check whether fixes generalize beyond the examples used to design them. Report the results with their denominators and identify which inputs were prepared manually or simulated.

Measure hard-condition errors, intent preservation by language, relevant titles that reach the shortlist, and appropriate clarification or abstention. Count a query as preserved when its decision-changing requirements, exclusions, negations and priorities agree with an acceptable interpretation or surface the expected uncertainty. Player comprehension and independently judged discovery quality are outside the current local pilot.
